# GAIVS Showcase — Recording Kit

Everything needed to record the 1–2 minute async showcase video of the results site.
Two cuts are provided: a **60-second** tight cut and a **2-minute** full cut.

- Page 1 — `index.html` — winners reveal (locked podium cards, Reveal button per place, applause + confetti)
- Page 2 — `hall.html` — 3D exhibition hall, 17 booths, winners podium at the end
- Present mode — add `?present=1` to the URL. `SPACE` advances a reveal / toggles hall auto-walk. `3` shows a 3-2-1 countdown. `c` hides the cursor.

Winner names are not final. Replace every `[3RD PLACE VENTURE]`, `[2ND PLACE VENTURE]`, `[1ST PLACE VENTURE]` before recording.

## 4. Recording setup (Windows)

### OBS Studio — quick config

**Settings → Video**
- Base (Canvas) Resolution: `1920x1080`
- Output (Scaled) Resolution: `1920x1080`
- Common FPS Value: `60`

**Settings → Output** (set Output Mode to Advanced → Recording tab)
- Type: Standard · Recording Format: `MP4` (or `MKV` + remux after, if you worry about crashes)
- Encoder: NVIDIA NVENC H.264 if available, otherwise x264
- Rate Control: `CBR`, Bitrate: `12000 Kbps` (plenty for 1080p60 with confetti and the 3D hall)
- x264 only: CPU Usage Preset `veryfast`

**Display Capture vs Window Capture**

| | Display Capture | Window Capture |
|---|---|---|
| Pros | Catches everything, no clipping, survives full-screen toggles and page navigation | Notifications and other windows can't appear in frame |
| Cons | A stray toast or taskbar popup lands on camera | Can go black on hardware-accelerated content; may drop the browser's 3D canvas; breaks if the window is re-created |

**Recommendation: use Display Capture**, and remove the risk instead by turning on Focus Assist and closing everything else (see checklist). The 3D hall is the exact content Window Capture is worst at.

**Capturing the site's applause and chime (this is the part people get wrong)**

The confetti applause and the winner chime come from the browser, so they must be in the recording as desktop audio.

1. Settings → Audio → Global Audio Devices:
   - **Desktop Audio** = your speakers/headphones (Default). This is what captures the site.
   - **Mic/Auxiliary Audio** = your microphone.
2. In the Audio Mixer, click the gear on each source → **Advanced Audio Properties**:
   - Mic/Aux → Channels/Downmix: **Mono** (a mono voice sits centered and clean)
   - Desktop Audio → leave **Stereo**
   - Set both to **Track 1** so they mix into one file.
3. Levels — aim your mic peaks at **−6 dB** and desktop audio peaks at **−18 to −20 dB**. Roughly: narration clearly on top, applause audible underneath. On the reveals, stop talking and let the applause come forward on its own.
4. Filters on the mic (right-click → Filters): Noise Suppression (RNNoise) + Compressor (ratio 4:1) + Limiter at −3 dB. Optional but makes a big difference on a laptop mic.
5. **Do a 10-second test recording and play it back** before the real take. Confirm you can hear both your voice AND the applause.

### No-OBS fallback

**Xbox Game Bar** (`Win + G`, then `Win + Alt + R` to start/stop)
- Records the *focused window only* — you cannot switch apps mid-recording, and it will not follow you across a desktop.
- Captures system audio + mic, but with far less control over the balance. Set Windows volume so the site's applause is not overwhelming before you start.
- Caps at 1080p30 on most machines and has a recording length limit (default 2 hours — fine here).
- It may refuse to record if it doesn't consider the browser a "game"; enable Settings → Gaming → Captures → "Record what happened".

**Browser tab recording** (Chrome/Edge built-in, or a screen-record extension)
- Simplest option, but resolution follows the tab size and quality is usually noticeably softer.
- Many extensions do NOT capture tab audio by default — if the applause is missing from your test file, this is why.
- Navigating from `index.html` to `hall.html` is fine within a tab recording; switching tabs is not.

**If all else fails:** record the two pages as two separate takes and join them. The cut from the podium to the hall is a natural edit point.

### Browser prep checklist

- [ ] Zoom set to exactly **100%** (`Ctrl + 0`)
- [ ] Bookmarks bar hidden (`Ctrl + Shift + B`)
- [ ] Extensions/profile avatars out of frame — use a clean profile or a guest window
- [ ] Load `index.html?present=1` and **complete the password unlock BEFORE you start recording** — the unlock is remembered for the browser session, so the gate must never appear on camera. Do not close the browser or open a new window between unlocking and recording.
- [ ] Full screen with `F11` **after** unlocking and after the page has loaded
- [ ] Press `c` once to hide the cursor
- [ ] Notifications off: Windows Settings → System → Notifications → **Do not disturb** ON. Quit Slack/Teams/Outlook/Discord.
- [ ] Have `hall.html?present=1` pre-loaded once in this session so assets are cached, then return to `index.html?present=1` for the take

## 5. Pre-flight checklist

1. Winner placeholders replaced with the final venture names in your script printout.
2. Password unlock done; gate confirmed gone; page reloads without prompting.
3. `?present=1` in the URL on both pages.
4. Zoom 100%, bookmarks hidden, `F11` full screen, cursor hidden with `c`.
5. Do not disturb ON; chat apps and mail quit.
6. Reveal state is **reset** — all three cards locked (use the "Reset reveal" control, then reload).
7. Hall visited once already this session so it loads fast on camera.
8. OBS at 1080p/60, Display Capture, Desktop Audio + Mic both on Track 1.
9. Ten-second test recording done and **played back** — voice and applause both audible.
10. Script open on a second screen or printed. Water nearby. Take a breath before pressing `3`.

## 6. Three mistakes to avoid

**1. Revealing too fast, before the applause lands.**
The confetti and applause need about two seconds to feel like a moment — three on first place. If you hit `SPACE` again while the previous card is still celebrating, the whole reveal reads as rushed and the audio turns into mush. Count silently: *one-one-thousand, two-one-thousand,* then speak.

**2. Narrating over the chime.**
The winner chime is the emotional peak of the video. Stop talking. Let it ring out completely, then start the line. This is why the beat budgets include explicit pauses — they are part of the timing, not slack.

**3. Starting the hall narration before the hall has loaded.**
`hall.html` needs a moment to build the 3D scene and 17 booths. If you talk over a black or half-built frame, you either waste your best line or have to re-record. Navigate, stay silent, wait until the entrance is fully drawn, **then** press `SPACE` and speak. Pre-loading the hall once (checklist item 7) makes this wait short and predictable.
